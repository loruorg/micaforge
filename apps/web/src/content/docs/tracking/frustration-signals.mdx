Turn it on with `data-frustration` on the script tag:

```html
<script defer data-site="1" data-frustration src="/mf.js"></script>
```

The tracker then loads the `frustration` plugin and reports three signals. They appear on
the **Frustration** screen, under Behavior.

## Rage clicks

Three or more clicks inside one second, within 30 pixels of each other. One report per
burst, with the element clicked and how many clicks the burst had. A triple click that
selects text is how people select a paragraph, so a burst that leaves a text selection on
something that is not a control is not counted.

## Dead clicks

A click on something that looks clickable (a link, a button, an element with a button,
link, tab or menu-item role, an `onclick` handler, or a `data-track` tag) that causes no
navigation, no change to the page and no network request within one second. Controls that
answer out of sight, such as a text field taking focus, a checkbox, a select, a video or a
download link, are never counted. Each element is reported at most once per page view.

To see whether a request started, the plugin notes the moment `fetch` or
`XMLHttpRequest` is called. It wraps both, changes nothing else about them, and does so
only on a page where `data-frustration` is set.

## Abandoned forms

A form somebody typed into or changed, then left without sending: the page was closed, or
the visitor navigated to another path. A focus alone does not start a form; tabbing
through a page is not filling it in.

The report names the form by its `id`, its `name`, or a short selector, and the **last
field touched** by its `name` or `id`. Never a value: nothing in the plugin reads what
was typed.

## How an element is named

By a short CSS selector built from tag names, stable ids and class names, at most three
levels deep: `section#pricing > div.card > button.buy`. Generated names (CSS-module hashes,
framework ids) are left out, because they change between builds. The page's text is never
used. Give an element an `id` or a `data-track` name and its selector becomes exactly that.

## Limits

At most 20 signals per page view. A signal is a `frustration` event named `rage_click`,
`dead_click` or `form_abandon`, so it passes the same gates as every other hit: opt-out,
`data-sample`, `data-exclude` and `data-mask`.

## From the API

`GET /api/stats/frustration` returns the totals, the pages with their pageviews, the
elements, and the forms with their last fields, under the standard
[query envelope](/docs/api/stats-api).
